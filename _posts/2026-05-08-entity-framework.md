## Overview

In Lab 9 of CSCI 340, we explored the Entity framework with Razor Pages. We went through a tutorial again, but this time we needed to change the name of the project to a name of our choice, and make a couple of changes to the tutorial flow. [This link](https://github.com/PaulBeggs/csci340lab9) will take you to my GitHub repository for my Razor Page project.

## The Tutorial

In the tutorial, the first step was basically doing Lab 8 all over again. The rest of the tutorial was concerned with adding more tables in the database, and handling functionality between models. It also focused a lot on user input verification, and getting nice displays in the `cshtml.cs` files, like the displaying of classes a table. In general, the easiest parts of the tutorial was following the dotnet commands, and obviously copy and pasting, even though I had to change the namespace, and some of the entity names. I really wish that I would have done more reading and comprehension of what the tutorial was trying to show me, as I can say in retrospect, it has made the final project much harder to understand.

When it comes to the parts that were confusing and different from the first tutorial, there are quite a few. The Entity framework basically provides huge chunks of copy and paste code, with some instruction on how the pieces fit together. In general, it was pretty confusing trying to learn these new relationships within the database with only small comments on how the pieces fit together. Again, perhaps the reason why it was so confusing was because I did the project over 2 main sittings, and didn't take enough time to fully comprehend what was going on. Instead, I was more focused on just getting the project done. This is definitely something I wish I could go back and do differently.

When it came to working with multiple tables, it kind of made sense how the primary keys and foreign keys were used to adjoin tables together to allow for querying and data manipulation. The parts that I struggled with the most had to do with concurrency within the tables, as that part of the tutorial just completely went over my head. 

In summary, I wish I would have spent more time on the tutorial, actually understanding what exactly the lines of code were doing, rather than getting the project done. I think if I would have done that, then my final project would have made more sense. In terms of how prepared I feel to make the project for the client, it's tough to say. On one hand, I can easily follow this tutorial again, and actually pay attention to what's going on, and try to replicate that with some major adjustments. However, on the other hand, since I was slow to start the final project, other team members were able to customize the whole layout according to what they felt the website should look like. Since their vision of the website consisted of functionality that wasn't covered on the tutorial, or was expanded to the $n$th degree from the tutorial, it was basically like grasping at straws trying to keep up. Therefore, I think time management was my biggest enemy for this project, and the final one.
